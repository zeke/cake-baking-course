# Prepare the batter: completion alternatives

These are alternative interaction treatments, not three consecutive hurdles. All refer to the same lesson and physical goal. The default lesson uses learner confirmation. A host may select another treatment, but must not silently substitute knowledge for doing the work.

## Shared state and media

Course: **Bake Your First Cake**. **2 of 6 complete**. Buy the ingredients and Preheat the oven are complete; Prepare the batter is active. Bake the cake, Decorate the cake, and Serve to guests are upcoming. This is an illustrative state, not a real learner record.

Agent: “Your ingredients are ready and the oven is preheated. Let's make the batter.”

Video card: **Know when to stop mixing**. Supporting copy: “Fold gently until no dry flour remains. A few small lumps are fine.” A still or storyboard is illustrative; no playable video exists yet.

## A. Learner confirmation

Agent: “Scrape the bottom of the bowl once more. Is there any dry flour left?”

Primary action: **My batter is ready**.

On confirmation: “Great. You've prepared the batter. Next, pour it into the pan and bake.”

Record learner-confirmed completion, not agent-observed completion. Allow “I need help” without advancing.

## B. Description and follow-up

Prompt: **Describe your batter**.

Learner example: “It's thick and pale, with a few small lumps.”

Agent: “Small lumps are fine. When you scrape the bottom of the bowl, can you still see any dry flour?”

Learner: “No, it's all mixed in.”

Agent: “Then stop mixing. Your batter is ready for the pan.”

If dry flour remains, suggest a few gentle folds and ask again. If something seems wrong, ask what was measured and added rather than declaring success. Completion waits for the learner's report that all ingredients are incorporated and no dry flour remains.

## C. Multiple-choice check

Question: **When should you stop mixing?**

- A. When no dry flour remains
- B. When the batter is perfectly smooth
- C. After five more minutes

Correct: A.

Feedback for A: “Exactly. A few small lumps are fine. Extra mixing can make the cake tougher.”

Feedback for B: “You don't need to remove every lump. Look for dry flour instead.”

Feedback for C: “Use the batter's appearance, not extra minutes. Stop once the dry flour is incorporated.”

After a correct answer, ask “Does your own batter have any dry flour left?” and wait for physical-task confirmation before completing the exercise. Wrong answers leave the step active and allow retry.
